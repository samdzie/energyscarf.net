*This post was written for the [September 2026 edition](/blog/midway-menagerie-september-2026-call-for-submissions) of [Midway Menagerie](https://lark.gay/furry-carnival), a furry blogging carnival.*

Am I starting my submission for this month's *Menagerie* less than four hours before it's due? Yes. Isn't this the month that I'm hosting it myself? Yes. Shouldn't I be setting a better example as the host? Perhaps. However you must consider that in seventeen years of school I never once figured out how to space out my assignments. So here we are.

For its entry in the first edition of *Midway Menagerie*, my friend Lark wrote a touching post titled ["It's not really about the animals"](https://lark.gay/log/its-not-about-the-animals/) about its experience discovering the local furry scene and what this community means to it. I briefly had the idea of writing a response called "It's exclusively about the animals" as a joke, but this line of thinking brought me to a deeper idea. How important are "the animals" to my furry identity and my self-identity as a whole?

Like many furries, I've long held an interest in stories featuring animal characters. I was obsessed with *Pokémon Mystery Dungeon* (PMD) and *Warrior Cats* as a kid, which funneled me into the furry fandom. *Animorphs* was another book series that captivated me. While it didn't feature anthropomorphic animals, the main characters held the ability to transform into animals for short periods of time as a tool to resist an alien invasion. My favorite character, Tobias, becomes trapped as a red-tailed hawk in the first book and although he regains the ability to morph later, he chooses to remain a hawk. Much of his arc in the series concerns his relationship with his humanity and its dissonance with his body. Unsurprisingly, he is widely read as a transgender allegory.[^1]

I spent much of 6th grade pretending to be Tobias, morphed as a human in two-hour increments before sneaking to the bathroom to demorph. I have a distinct memory of standing in my family's backyard late one night watching the trees sway in the cold autumn wind and envisioning myself as a hawk, hunkering down in the branches. At some point, I snapped out of my daydream and realized that this fantasy could never be realized. I was stuck in my human body and no one was coming to rescue me from dreary reality.

I daydreamed extensively for most of my childhood and teenage years, imagining myself living in the worlds of the books I read and games I played. The PMD world was a favorite, one I inhabited most of high school. One day, it occurred to me that most people spend all of their time in the real world. The thought of living like that&mdash;that this was the way I was expected to be&mdash;filled me with dread.

Even in adulthood, people referring to me as a creature of some kind has brought me much satisfaction. Another member of a Discord server I joined in college, upon seeing my Shinx icon amongst the anime pfps of the other members, remarked that I looked like the pet of the group. More recently, a number of my friends occasionally refer to me as a dog or puppy, which always lights me up. Whether this appeals to me in a furry way, a kink way, or a potentially therian way is something I'm still trying to sort out.

So could I be therian? I'm not really sure. I attended an informational panel/howl (therian gathering) hosted by [Hearth](https://bsky.app/profile/hearthfox.bsky.social) at ANE 2023 and found some of the experiences shared by them and other panel attendees to be relatable. A number of my fellow PMD fans identify as therians and I've shared some of their longing to live in that world. On the other paw, I can't claim to have the sufficient experience to call myself an animal-lover like other people can. Despite having a dog sona, I never had a pet dog myself and in fact was afraid of dogs for most of my childhood.[^2] I'm also too content with the amenities of living in human society&mdash;if not the nightmare that is living under capitalism&mdash;to say that I'd rather live in the wild like many therians do.

Ideally, I would end this post with some conclusive insights about my personal identity or the way furries in general integrate furriness into their self-conception. Unfortunately a month of contemplating this topic has brought me no hard answers, though it at least has been a valuable period of reflection. I'll continue to think and perhaps provide updates on this blog in the future. Regardless, I have a deeper appreciation for the role pretending to be an animal on the internet has played in understanding myself.

[^1]: I agree with the reading but not with the usual "trapped in the wrong body" interpretation, but that's a discussion that warrants its own blog post.

[^2]: This began to change after I met my cousin's dog Lucy, a golden retriever, who was characteristically relaxed and friendly. It turns out that I was mostly afraid of poorly trained dogs, and it seems that many dog owners are poor trainers.
